What a cloud PBX is (no box, no wiring, no technician)

An old-style office phone system — what most people in Pakistan still call a PABX, EPABX or telephone exchange — is a physical unit sitting in your office, cabled to desk phones, installed and serviced by an engineer. A hosted cloud PBX removes all of that. The exchange runs online, so:

  • No box on the wall to buy, power or repair.
  • No wiring pulled through the office for each new seat.
  • No technician booked for install, moves or changes — you manage it yourself, or we do it for you.

All the intelligence — the greeting that answers callers, the extensions that ring the right person, the recordings and reports — lives in software. Your team simply signs into the app. If you want the full plain-English background first, our guide on what a PBX is walks through one real call from start to finish.

Why cloud PBX beats an on-prem PABX

The displacement story is simple: an on-prem PABX is a large one-time purchase plus ongoing service, while a cloud PBX is a small predictable monthly fee. In the local market, legacy hardware runs to serious money — a Panasonic NS-500 is seen around PKR 224,999 and a TES-824 around PKR 114,999, with Grandstream IP phones from roughly PKR 10,700 to 40,000 each (these are third-party market rates, not voipcom prices). Before any of that rings a single call, you also pay for cabling and an engineer.

Who a cloud PBX works for

It fits any Pakistani business that answers customers by phone and wants to stop losing calls to a drawer full of personal SIMs. Common cases include retail and wholesale shops, clinics and salons taking bookings, property and travel offices, and fast-growing call-centre and BPO teams (an export sector growing roughly 24% year on year). Online sellers pair the cloud PBX with automated Shopify COD confirmation calls to cut return-to-origin on cash-on-delivery orders. Whatever the trade, it is the backbone of a proper business phone system in Pakistan — available in Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad and nationwide.

FAQ

Cloud PBX Pakistan — common questions

What is a cloud PBX and how does it work in Pakistan?

A cloud PBX is a business phone exchange that runs in our cloud instead of a box on your wall. Your team answers one business number from a mobile or desktop app — the IVR greeting, extensions, queues, recording and reports all live online. No hardware sits in your office.

How much does a cloud PBX cost in Pakistan?

voipcom cloud PBX starts from PKR 2,900/month for the Starter plan (1 city number, 3 extensions), billed in PKR with no hardware to buy. The Team plan is PKR 7,500/month (2 numbers, 10 extensions) and Business is custom. See the pricing page for details.

Do I need to buy any hardware for a hosted PBX?

No. A hosted PBX needs no box, no cabling and no technician visit. The app runs on the smartphones and laptops your team already has. Desk phones are optional, not required.

Can I get a Karachi or Lahore number with a cloud PBX?

Yes. We provide Pakistan DID city numbers — Karachi 021, Lahore 042, Islamabad 051, Faisalabad 041 and Multan 061 — allocated through licensed operators. The number is owned by your business, not stuck in an employee's SIM.

Is a cloud PBX legal in Pakistan?

Yes. In Pakistan, public voice and DID numbers are provided through licensed LL/LDI operators. voipcom works with licensed operators and stays PTA/PSEB-aligned. We are not a licensed carrier ourselves — we set up and manage the cloud PBX on top of properly allocated numbers.

How long does it take to set up a cloud PBX?

Usually one working day. We set up your number, record your IVR greeting and create your extensions, then show your team the app. Most of the time goes into deciding what the greeting should say.
