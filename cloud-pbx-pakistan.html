A cloud PBX in Pakistan is a business phone system where the exchange runs in our cloud rather than in a box bolted to your office wall. Your team answers one business number from a mobile or desktop app, whether they're in the shop, in the car or at home. Key takeaway

A cloud PBX gives you a proper company phone system (IVR greeting, unlimited extensions, recording and reports) without owning any equipment. You pay a monthly fee in PKR and your team answers everything from an app.

What a cloud PBX is (no box, no wiring, no technician)

The old-style office phone system, what most people in Pakistan still call a PABX, EPABX or telephone exchange, is a physical unit in your office, cabled to desk phones and installed and serviced by an engineer. A hosted cloud PBX does away with all of that. The exchange runs online, which means:

  • No box on the wall to buy, power or repair.
  • No wiring to pull through the office every time you add a seat.
  • No technician to book for installs, moves or changes. You can manage it yourself, or ask us and we'll do it for you.

All the clever parts (the greeting that answers callers, the extensions that ring the right person, the recordings and reports) live in software. Your team just signs into the app. If you'd like the plain-English background first, our guide on what a PBX is follows one real call from start to finish.

Why cloud PBX beats an on-prem PABX

The sums are simple. An on-prem PABX is a big one-time purchase plus ongoing servicing, while a cloud PBX is a small monthly fee you can predict. Legacy hardware in the local market isn't cheap: a Panasonic NS-500 is listed around PKR 224,999 and a TES-824 around PKR 114,999, and Grandstream IP phones run roughly PKR 10,700 to 40,000 each (those are third-party market rates, not voipcom prices). And before any of it rings a single call, you've also paid for cabling and an engineer.

FAQ

Cloud PBX Pakistan: common questions

What is a cloud PBX and how does it work in Pakistan?

A cloud PBX is a business phone exchange that runs in our cloud instead of in a box on your office wall. Your team answers one business number from a mobile or desktop app. The IVR greeting, extensions, queues, recording and reports all live online, so nothing sits in your office.

How much does a cloud PBX cost in Pakistan?

voipcom cloud PBX starts at PKR 3,200 a month for the Starter plan: 1 extension, a 051 number, call recording, IVR setup and the mobile and desktop apps, billed in PKR with no hardware to buy. Extra extensions are priced per extension, with volume pricing for 5 or more, and Business plans are custom. The pricing page has the details.

Do I need to buy any hardware for a hosted PBX?

No. With a hosted PBX there is nothing to install, no cabling to run and no technician to book. The app runs on the smartphones and laptops your team already has. Desk phones are optional if you want them, never required.

Can I get a Karachi or Lahore number with a cloud PBX?

Not quite yet. Today we provide Islamabad 051 numbers, allocated through PTA-licensed operators. Karachi 021, Lahore 042 and mobile numbers are launching next, and Faisalabad 041 and Multan 061 are planned after that. See Pakistan DID city numbers for the current list. Whichever you take, the number belongs to your business, not to an employee's SIM.

Is a cloud PBX legal in Pakistan?

Yes. In Pakistan, public voice service and DID numbers come through licensed LL and LDI operators. voipcom works with those PTA-licensed operators and stays PTA and PSEB aligned. We are not a licensed carrier ourselves. We set up and manage the cloud PBX on top of numbers that have been allocated properly.

How long does it take to set up a cloud PBX?

Usually one working day. We set up your number, record your IVR greeting and create your extensions, then walk your team through the app. Most of that day goes on deciding what the greeting should say.
