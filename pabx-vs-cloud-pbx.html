If you are weighing PABX vs cloud PBX for your business, you are really asking one question: should the telephone exchange be a box you buy and bolt to the wall, or a service that runs in the cloud and rings on the phones you already own? Both route calls for a company. They just cost money — and behave — in completely different ways. This page lays out the difference between PABX and cloud PBX in plain English, with a full comparison table and the real numbers, so a Pakistani SME owner can decide in ten minutes.

If you are brand new to the term, start with what a PBX is and come back — the rest of this page assumes you know it is simply the system that lets a business share phone lines and answer one number.

PABX vs cloud PBX — the short answer

A PABX (also written EPABX) is an on-premise hardware exchange: a physical box installed in your office, wired to every desk, programmed by a technician. A cloud PBX does the exact same job, but the exchange is hosted in the provider's cloud — there is no box, no wiring and no technician. Your whole team answers one business number from a mobile or desktop app, from anywhere.

For most small and mid-size Pakistani businesses, the cloud PBX wins: it is cheaper to start, cheaper to run, and it follows your people instead of trapping your phone system in one building. The PABX still fits a few specific cases, which we cover honestly below.

What a PABX (on-premise) is and its real costs

A PABX is the machine every installer in Pakistan has fitted for twenty years. You buy the exchange, run structured cabling to each desk, buy a handset per seat, pay a technician to install and program it, add a UPS so load shedding does not kill your calls, and then pay a yearly maintenance contract on top. At 2026 market rates the box alone runs from roughly PKR 24,999–64,999 for a small Chinese exchange, about PKR 114,999 for a Panasonic KX-TES824, and around PKR 224,999 for a Panasonic KX-NS500 hybrid — and that is third-party hardware, not voipcom's. We break the full bill down on PABX price in Pakistan.

The deeper problem is not the sticker. A PABX is tied to one office. It cannot follow a salesman into the field or a support agent working from home, and when a staff member leaves, whatever lived on that desk phone tends to leave with them. Modern on-prem exchanges have improved — see IP PBX systems — but the box still sits in your building and depends on your power and your walls.

What a cloud PBX is

A cloud PBX moves the exchange off your wall and into the cloud. There is nothing to buy and nothing to install. Your team answers one professional business number from an app on the smartphones they already carry — office in your pocket. Add a new staff member and you create an extension in minutes; no cable, no call-out. The number is a DID owned by your business, so it survives an office move, a power cut or a resignation.

When on-premise PABX still makes sense (being fair)

A cloud PBX is not the right answer for absolutely everyone, and we would rather tell you that than lose your trust. An on-premise PABX still fits when:

  • You have no reliable internet. A cloud PBX needs a steady connection. A remote site with frequent, long outages and no backup link may be better served by a local box.
  • Policy forces on-site only. Some organisations have strict rules that voice must stay inside the building, on infrastructure they physically control.
  • You already own heavy analogue plant. A large site with cabling and dozens of working handsets already in the ground may keep the box a while longer — often alongside a modern IP PBX or cloud line for mobile staff.

Outside those cases, the box is usually solving a problem you no longer have.

Which should a Pakistani SMB choose?

For a shop, a clinic, an e-commerce team or a growing office, the cloud PBX is the clear pick. You skip the upfront cost, you add staff in minutes, your team answers one number from anywhere, and your business keeps its number through every move, power cut and resignation. The PABX earns its place only where reliable internet is genuinely absent or a policy demands an on-site box.

Can I keep my number if I switch to cloud PBX?

Yes. With a cloud PBX the business number is a DID owned by your company, not tied to a SIM or a desk phone. It survives an office move, a power cut and staff turnover, and it is the one number your whole team answers from the app.

Is cloud PBX legal in Pakistan?

Yes. Public voice and DID numbers in Pakistan are provided through licensed LL and LDI operators. voipcom works with licensed operators and is PTA and PSEB aligned; we are not a carrier ourselves — we deliver the cloud PBX and city numbers on top of licensed infrastructure.

No. Many businesses keep an existing PABX running while moving new numbers, mobile staff and order-confirmation calls onto a cloud PBX, then retire the box when the maintenance contract ends.
