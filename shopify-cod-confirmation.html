Why COD orders come back

Return-to-origin (RTO), the parcel that travels all the way to the customer and comes right back, commonly runs 20 to 35% of COD orders in Pakistan. Every one of those costs you twice. You pay the courier both ways, your stock sits in transit for a week, and your cash flow takes the hit. The usual culprits are predictable:

  • Fake and prank orders: a wrong number or a made-up name that was never going to open the door.
  • Buyer's remorse: ordered on impulse, cooled off by delivery day.
  • Duplicate orders: the customer clicked twice, or ordered the same thing from two of your ads.
  • Unreachable customers: the phone is off, or they never picked up the rider's call.

The old fix is to have staff phone each order by hand. At 50 orders a day that's hours of dialling, and it still misses the late-night rush. A COD robo call does the same job in seconds, on every order, without anyone picking up a handset.

How the order confirmation call works

Connect your Shopify store once. After that it runs on its own for every order:

  1. New COD order lands. Shopify tells voipcom the moment checkout completes.
  2. The customer's phone rings. A recorded prompt plays from your business number: "Thank you for your order from [your store]. Press 1 to confirm, press 2 to cancel."
  3. They press a key. 1 confirms, 2 cancels. If nobody answers, it retries, so a missed first ring isn't a lost order.
  4. The result is logged and synced. The order is tagged confirmed or cancelled back in Shopify, with a timestamp, so your packing team only sees orders worth shipping.

Because it runs on the same platform as your Pakistan DID number and IVR menus, the confirmation call comes from a real business line you own, rather than a random mobile SIM the customer will ignore. If a customer wants to talk to a person, the same number handles that call too.

Key takeaway: confirming before dispatch means the courier only carries parcels that will be accepted. That one filter is what takes a 30% RTO down to 18 to 22%, and it costs a fraction of one returned order.
