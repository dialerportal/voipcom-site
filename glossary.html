Core phone-system terms

PBX

Private Branch Exchange. The switchboard that connects your team's phones to each other and to the outside world, so a whole business shares one number with many internal lines. Old ones were metal boxes on the wall; today the smart version runs in the cloud. Start with our guide to what a PBX is.

PABX

Private Automatic Branch Exchange. The word most Pakistani buyers still use for a traditional on-premise phone box — a Panasonic, NEC or Chinese unit wired to desk phones in the office. "Automatic" simply means it switches calls without a human operator. See how it compares in PABX vs cloud PBX.

EPABX

Electronic Private Automatic Branch Exchange. The same thing as a PABX; the "E" only signals it is electronic rather than mechanical. In practice, PABX and EPABX mean the identical hardware exchange. Curious what those boxes cost? See PABX price in Pakistan.

IP PBX

Internet Protocol PBX. A PBX that carries calls as internet data (IP) instead of over copper wires. It can sit as a box in your office or run fully in the cloud, and it connects to SIP trunks rather than analogue lines. Read more about IP PBX systems.

Cloud PBX / Hosted PBX

The exchange runs in the provider's cloud, not your building. No box, no wiring, no technician visit. Your whole team answers one business number from a mobile or desktop app, anywhere. How calls are routed

Extension

A short internal number — like 101 or 102 — for one person or desk. Callers reach the right team member without anyone needing a separate SIM, and staff dial each other for free inside the system.

DID

Direct Inward Dialing. A real public phone number that rings straight into your system — a Karachi 021, Lahore 042, Islamabad 051, Faisalabad 041 or Multan 061 line. The number is owned by the business, not stuck in an employee's SIM. Browse Pakistan DID numbers.

IVR

Interactive Voice Response. The recorded menu that greets callers — "press 1 for sales, press 2 for support" — and routes them automatically. You can record it in Urdu, English or both. See the IVR system page.

Auto-attendant

Another name for the IVR's welcome greeting and menu: the automated receptionist that answers on the first ring, day or night, so your business never sounds busy and no call is missed.

Call queue

When every agent is busy, callers wait in an orderly line with hold music instead of hearing a busy tone. The next free agent picks up the next caller — fair for customers, calm for staff.

The VoIP technology

VoIP

Voice over Internet Protocol. Making phone calls over the internet instead of traditional copper lines. It is the underlying technology that lets a cloud PBX turn any stable 4G or broadband connection into a full business phone line.

SIP

Session Initiation Protocol. The common "language" that VoIP phones and apps use to start, ring and end a call. When someone mentions a "SIP account" or "SIP extension," they mean a line that speaks this standard.

SIP trunk

A virtual bundle of phone lines delivered over the internet that connects your PBX to the public phone network through a licensed operator. It replaces the old physical wires and can carry many simultaneous calls on one connection.

Trunk

The connection between your phone system and the outside world. On old PABX gear it was a physical line from the telephone exchange; on modern systems it is a SIP trunk running over the internet.

DTMF

Dual-Tone Multi-Frequency. The beep tones your phone makes when you press keypad digits. It is how a caller "presses 1 to confirm" inside an IVR — or on an automated COD order-confirmation call.

Softphone

An app on an ordinary smartphone or laptop that works just like an office desk phone. Your team makes and takes business calls from anywhere — your office in your pocket, no hardware to buy.

Records & e-commerce

Call recording

Every call saved as an audio file you can play back later — ideal for training new staff, checking quality and settling "who said what" without an argument. You choose which calls to record and keep.

CDR

Call Detail Record. The log line for each call: who called, which number, who answered, the time and the duration. Stacked together, CDRs become the reports you use to manage the team and spot missed calls.

COD

Cash on Delivery. The pay-when-it-arrives method behind most Pakistani e-commerce orders — roughly 75–95% of them. Because nothing is paid upfront, confirming each order by phone genuinely protects your margin. See Shopify COD confirmation calls.

RTO

Return to Origin. A COD parcel that is refused or undelivered and shipped back to the seller — pure loss on two-way shipping. RTO commonly runs about 20–35%; an automated confirmation call (press 1 confirm, 2 cancel) can pull it toward 18–22%. Learn how to cut COD returns.

FAQ

Questions people search

What is the difference between PABX and PBX?

PBX is the general term for a business phone exchange. PABX (and EPABX) is the older word Pakistani buyers use for a traditional on-premise box wired to desk phones. A cloud PBX is the same idea, but the exchange runs over the internet with no hardware.

What does DID mean in a phone system?

DID stands for Direct Inward Dialing: a real public number — such as a Karachi 021 or Lahore 042 line — that rings straight into your system. The DID is owned by the business, so it never leaves in an employee's SIM.

Is VoIP the same as SIP?

No. VoIP is the broad idea of carrying voice calls over the internet. SIP is the specific protocol, or language, that most VoIP phones and apps use to set up and end those calls. SIP is one of the standards that makes VoIP work.

What is the difference between IVR and auto-attendant?

They usually mean the same thing: the recorded greeting and menu that answers a call and routes it. Auto-attendant emphasises the automated-receptionist role, while IVR emphasises the press-a-key interaction. See our IVR system page.

What does RTO mean in COD e-commerce?

RTO means Return to Origin: a Cash on Delivery parcel that is refused or undelivered and shipped back to the seller, costing shipping both ways. An automated confirmation call — press 1 to confirm, 2 to cancel — helps reduce it.

What is a softphone?

A softphone is an app on a smartphone or laptop that works like an office desk phone. Your team makes and receives business calls, sees extensions and transfers callers — all without any physical phone.
