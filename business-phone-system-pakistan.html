What a modern business phone system is

Most small businesses in Pakistan still run on a drawer full of personal SIMs — one per employee, a separate bill for each, and the number walking out the door when the staff member leaves. A proper business phone system fixes that by making the number belong to the business, not the person. Callers dial one line; a recorded greeting answers on the first ring; the call reaches whoever is free; and it is all recorded and reported. If you want the idea explained end-to-end with one real call, start with our friendly guide on what a PBX is, and keep the telephony glossary open for any word that trips you up.

Your options: PABX, IP PBX and cloud PBX

The words matter here, because buyers in Pakistan search for all three. An old-style PABX — also called EPABX, telephone exchange or intercom — is a physical box wired to desk phones and serviced by an engineer. An IP PBX is a modern system that carries calls over the internet, giving you on-premise-style control with far more flexibility. A cloud PBX (or hosted PBX) goes furthest: the exchange runs entirely in our cloud, so there is no box at all and your team answers from an app anywhere.

Why most SMBs pick cloud

The deciding factor is usually cost and speed. An on-prem PABX is a large one-time purchase before it rings a single call — in the local market a Panasonic NS-500 is seen around PKR 224,999 and a TES-824 around PKR 114,999, with Grandstream IP phones from roughly PKR 10,700 to 40,000 each (these are third-party market rates, not voipcom prices), plus cabling and a technician. A cloud PBX turns that into a small monthly fee with nothing to install. It also answers the very Pakistani realities of load shedding and staff on the move: calls forward to a mobile if the internet drops, and the app means nobody is chained to a desk. For fast-growing call-centre and BPO teams — an export sector growing roughly 24% year on year — the cloud also scales in minutes instead of an engineer visit.

The pieces that make it work

Whichever type you choose, a business phone system is built from the same handful of parts. On voipcom every one of these is included on every plan, not sold as an add-on:

  • City numbers (DIDs) — your own Pakistan DID number in Karachi 021, Lahore 042, Islamabad 051, Faisalabad 041 or Multan 061, owned by the business rather than an employee's SIM.
  • Extensions — short internal numbers like 101 and 102 so callers reach the right person, with no extra SIMs to buy.
  • IVR greeting — a recorded welcome menu ("press 1 for sales, press 2 for support") in Urdu, English or both. See the IVR system page.
  • Call recording & reports — every call saved as audio, and a dashboard showing who called, when, who answered and for how long.
  • The app — a softphone on the smartphones and laptops your team already owns; your office in your pocket, anywhere with stable 4G or broadband.
  • Order-confirmation calls — for online sellers, automated Shopify COD confirmation calls (press 1 to confirm, 2 to cancel) that help cut COD returns on cash-on-delivery orders.

Cash on delivery dominates Pakistani e-commerce — roughly 75–95% of orders — and return-to-origin (RTO) commonly runs 20–35%. An automated confirmation call can pull RTO down toward 18–22%, which is why so many stores bolt COD calls onto their phone system.

No. Any stable 4G or broadband connection is enough — a voice call uses less data than a WhatsApp voice note. If the internet drops, calls can automatically forward to a mobile number, so load shedding or a dead router never means losing a customer.
