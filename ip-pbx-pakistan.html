What an IP PBX system is

An IP PBX system is the exchange that runs your business phones — the part that greets a caller, offers a menu, and connects them to the right person — except it moves calls over the internet instead of over copper wires. In Pakistan you will hear the older wired version called a PABX, EPABX, telephone exchange or intercom. An IP PBX does the same job in a more modern way: the "PBX" is the brain, and "IP" simply means the calls travel as internet data.

That one change unlocks everything a growing business wants. Because calls are data, your whole team can answer one business number from a mobile or desktop app, whether they sit in Karachi, Lahore or at home. You get a proper IVR receptionist, extensions for each person, call transfers, queues and call recording — the same features large companies pay lakhs for — without a machine bolted to your wall. Key takeaway: An IP PBX is a phone exchange that runs over the internet. A hosted IP PBX puts that exchange in our cloud — so you get every business feature with no box, no wiring, and no technician, from PKR 3,200/month.

IP PBX vs the old PABX

The traditional PABX was a physical box wired to fixed phone lines, sitting in a cupboard in your office. It worked, but it locked you in place: calls only rang on the desk phones wired to it, adding a line meant a technician, and if the office had a power or line fault, the whole system went quiet. When people search PABX price in Pakistan, they are pricing that box — hardware, cards, handsets and installation, all paid up front.

An IP PBX keeps the useful part — the smart call handling — and drops the constraints. Numbers are not tied to a SIM or a socket; they are yours as a business, and they ring wherever your team is logged in. Adding a new staff member is a setting, not a site visit. On-prem IP PBX vs hosted cloud

Here is the fork most Pakistani buyers miss: even within "IP PBX", there are two very different paths.

An on-premise IP PBX is still a box you buy and own — a Yeastar or 3CX appliance, for example — that you install, license, secure and maintain yourself. It is more modern than an old PABX, but you are still the IT department: patching firmware, renewing licenses, and troubleshooting when something breaks.

A hosted (cloud) IP PBX flips that. The exchange runs in our cloud. There is nothing to install, nothing to secure on your side, and no license to renew — you simply log in to the app. This is where hosted wins for small and medium business: you get the enterprise features of an IP PBX without the enterprise headache, and you pay a predictable monthly fee in PKR instead of a big cheque up front. For most SMBs — a Shopify store, a clinic, a real-estate office, a small BPO — hosted is the obvious fit. You do not want to become a phone-system administrator; you want the phones to work and the reports to be honest. Hardware you do and don't need

This is the question that stops most purchases: "what do I have to buy?" With a hosted IP PBX, the honest answer is almost nothing.

You do not need: a PABX box, PRI/analogue line cards, structured cabling, or a per-employee SIM. You do not need an on-site server or a rack. The exchange lives in our cloud.

You do need: a working internet connection and the phones your team already owns. Everyone answers the business number through our free mobile or desktop app — that is the whole setup for most stores and offices.

Optional, not required: desk IP phones. If you run a busy reception or a call floor and prefer a physical handset with a headset, IP phones (from brands like Grandstream) plug straight in. In the market these run roughly PKR 10,700–40,000 each — a one-time cost you can add later, or skip entirely. The point of a hosted IP PBX is that the app works on day one, so hardware becomes a choice, not a barrier.

IP PBX questions Pakistani buyers ask

What is an IP PBX system?

An IP PBX is a phone exchange that connects calls over the internet instead of copper lines. It does everything an old PABX did — menus, extensions, transfers, recording — but because the calls travel as data, your team can answer from a mobile or desktop app anywhere.

What is the difference between a PABX and an IP PBX?

A PABX or EPABX is the older on-premise telephone exchange wired to physical lines. An IP PBX runs the same call handling over the internet. A hosted IP PBX goes one step further — the exchange lives in our cloud, so there is no box, no wiring and no technician visit. See PABX vs cloud PBX for the full comparison.

Do I need to buy IP phones for an IP PBX?

No. With a hosted IP PBX your team answers from a free mobile or desktop app. Desk IP phones from brands like Grandstream are optional — handy for a busy reception or a call floor, but never required to go live.
