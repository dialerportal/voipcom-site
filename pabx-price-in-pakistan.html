If you are searching for the PABX price in Pakistan, you have probably been quoted anywhere from twenty-five thousand rupees to well over two lakh — and you are wondering why the range is so wide. The short answer is that "PABX" covers everything from a cheap Chinese intercom box to a proper Panasonic hybrid exchange, and the sticker price is only a fraction of what you actually spend over the life of the system. This page lays out the real 2026 market rates, then shows you the costs that never make it onto the first quote.

A quick note on words, because buyers use them all differently. The old on-premise box in your store room is a PABX, EPABX, telephone exchange or intercom. The modern versions are called an IP PBX or a cloud PBX. They all do the same job — route calls for a business — but they cost money in very different ways.

When people say "PABX" in Pakistan they very often mean Panasonic, because it is the brand every installer has fitted for twenty years. The hidden costs nobody quotes you

The box price is where the conversation starts, not where it ends. Before you compare a PABX to anything, add these in — because a salesman rarely will:

  • Structured cabling. A wire has to run from the exchange to every desk. In an existing building that means drilling, trunking and a cabling crew.
  • Installation and programming. A technician sets up the exchange, records the greeting and programs the extensions — a day rate you pay again for every change later.
  • Handsets. One per desk. Grandstream IP phones run roughly PKR 10,700 to 40,000 each at market rates, and that scales with your team.
  • Power and backup. The box needs a UPS so load shedding does not kill your phones mid-call.
  • Annual maintenance. Most installers sell a yearly contract on top, plus call-out charges when something breaks.
  • It dies in one building. A PABX cannot follow a salesman to the field or a support agent working from home. When staff leave, whatever lived on that desk phone leaves too.

Key takeaway: the honest cost of a PABX is the box plus cabling plus handsets plus a technician plus a UPS plus a maintenance contract — most of which never appears on the first quote. Judge it on the total, not the sticker.
