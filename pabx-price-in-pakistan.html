If you're searching for the PABX price in Pakistan, you've probably been quoted anything from twenty-five thousand rupees to well over two lakh, and you're wondering why the range is so wide. The short answer: "PABX" covers everything from a cheap Chinese intercom box to a proper Panasonic hybrid exchange, and the sticker price is only part of what you'll spend over the life of the system. This page lays out the real 2026 market rates, then walks you through the costs that never make it onto the first quote.

A quick note on words, because everyone uses them a little differently. The old on-premise box in your store room is a PABX, EPABX, telephone exchange or intercom. The modern versions are called an IP PBX or a cloud PBX. They all do the same job, which is routing calls for a business, but the way you pay for them is very different.

Panasonic PABX price, and why it's the benchmark

When people in Pakistan say "PABX" they usually mean Panasonic, because it's the brand every installer has been fitting for twenty years. The hidden costs nobody quotes you

The box price is where the conversation starts. Before you compare a PABX with anything else, add these in, because the salesman rarely will:

  • Structured cabling. A wire has to run from the exchange to every desk. In a building that's already finished, that means drilling, trunking and a cabling crew.
  • Installation and programming. A technician sets up the exchange, records the greeting and programs the extensions. That's a day rate, and you pay it again every time you need a change.
  • Handsets. One per desk. Grandstream IP phones cost roughly PKR 10,700 to 40,000 each at market rates, and the bill grows with your team.
  • Power and backup. The box needs a UPS, otherwise load shedding cuts your phones off mid-call.
  • Annual maintenance. Most installers sell a yearly contract on top, plus call-out charges when something breaks.
  • It stays in one building. A PABX can't follow a salesman into the field or a support agent working from home. And when someone leaves, whatever lived on that desk phone goes with them.

Key takeaway: the real cost of a PABX is the box plus cabling, handsets, a technician, a UPS and a maintenance contract. Most of that never appears on the first quote, so judge it on the total rather than the sticker.
