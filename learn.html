PBX 101

The phone system, explained like a friend would

PBX stands for "private branch exchange" — but forget that. The simple idea is one number for your business that works intelligently. There is no hard jargon on this page: every term is explained in plain words, the way you would explain it to someone over a cup of tea.

How it works

What a PBX actually is

The easiest way to understand it is to follow one real call from start to finish.

  1. 01

    A customer dials your one number

    Your business has a single city number — say a Karachi 021 line or a Lahore 042 line. No matter how many people you have working, the customer only has to remember this one number.

  2. 02

    A recorded greeting answers instantly

    "Welcome to voipcom. Press 1 for sales, press 2 for support." That menu is called an IVR — it answers on the first ring every single time, even at 2 in the morning. Your business sounds bigger and is never busy.

  3. 03

    The call reaches the right people

    Press 1 and the call goes to your sales team's extensions — small internal numbers like 101 or 102. If several people work together, the caller waits in a queue and the next free person picks up. Staff answer wherever they are, on a desk phone or on the mobile app.

  4. 04

    Every call is logged and recorded

    Who called, when, who answered, how long it took — it is all saved in reports you can open whenever you like. Calls can be recorded too, so if a customer says "you promised me this," you can simply play it back and listen.

Quick glossary

The terms, in plain words

Six words you will hear about business phone systems — each in one simple sentence.

DID — your number

A phone number that lives in the cloud, not on a SIM card. It stays with your business forever — say an 021, 042, or 051 city number.

Extension — one person

A short internal number for each member of the team. 101 is Ahmed, 102 is Sana. Callers reach the right person without a new SIM.

IVR — the greeting menu

The recorded voice that picks up the call and offers options — "press 1 for sales, press 2 for support." Record it in Urdu, in English, or in both.

Queue — a fair line

When everyone is busy, callers wait their turn with hold music instead of hearing a busy tone. The next free agent takes the next caller.

Call recording

Every call is saved as an audio file you can play back. Perfect for training new staff and settling "who said what" without an argument.

Softphone — the app

An app on an ordinary smartphone that works just like an office desk phone. Your team makes and takes business calls from anywhere — your office in your pocket.

The old way vs the Voipcom way

Most small Pakistani businesses run on a pile of personal SIMs. Here is what changes when the phone system belongs to the business instead of the staff.

The old way

  • One SIM per employee — a drawer full of numbers, and a separate monthly bill for each one
  • When an employee leaves, the number leaves with them — along with the customers who called it
  • No record of anything — missed calls, promises, and complaints all disappear
  • The landline is tied to a desk — nobody answers after hours or from home

The Voipcom way

  • One business number, endless extensions — add a new person in minutes, no SIM needed
  • The number is owned by the business — staff come and go, customers keep dialling the same number
  • Every call logged and recorded — reports show exactly who answered what, and when
  • The app puts your office in your pocket — answer from the shop, the car, or from home

Straight answers

Honest questions

The things business owners really ask us — answered without any sales gloss.

Do I have to buy new phones?

No. The app runs on the smartphones your team already has. Desk phones are optional — some owners like a nice one at reception, but nobody forces you to buy any hardware.

Does it need very fast internet?

No. Any stable 4G or broadband connection works. A voice call uses less data than sending a WhatsApp voice note — the quality of the connection matters more than raw speed.

Can I keep my current number?

Bring it to the demo and we will look at what is possible with that specific number — it varies from case to case, and we will tell you the honest answer before you decide anything.

Can the greeting be in Urdu?

Yes. Record your IVR greeting in Urdu, in English, or in both — for example, a welcome in Urdu and the menu options in English. You can change the recording whenever you like.

What if the internet goes down?

Calls can automatically forward to any mobile number you choose. Load shedding or a dead router should never mean losing a customer.

How long does setup take?

Usually one working day. We set up your number, greeting, and extensions, then show your team the app — most of your time goes into deciding what the IVR should say.

Does my staff need SIMs for this?

No. Extensions ring inside the app over the internet. No SIMs to buy, no monthly package per employee, and the customer never sees anyone's personal number.

Setup times are typical, not a firm guarantee — every business is a little different. Ask us anything at the demo.
