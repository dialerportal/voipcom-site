PBX 101

What is a PBX? Explained the way a friend would

PBX stands for "private branch exchange". You can forget that straight away. The idea underneath it is simple: one number for your whole business, and some sense about where each call should go. There's no hard jargon on this page. Every term gets explained in plain words, the way you'd explain it to a friend over chai.

How it works

What a PBX actually is

The easiest way to get it is to follow one real call from start to finish.

  1. 01

    A customer dials your one number

    Your business has a single city number, say an Islamabad 051 line. It doesn't matter whether you have three people or thirty. The customer only has to remember this one number.

  2. 02

    A recorded greeting picks up straight away

    "Welcome to voipcom. Press 1 for sales, press 2 for support." That menu is called an IVR. It answers on the first ring, every time, even at 2 in the morning. Your business sounds bigger than it is, and nobody ever gets a busy tone.

  3. 03

    The call reaches the right people

    Press 1 and the call rings your sales team's extensions, which are just short internal numbers like 101 or 102. If a few people share the work, the caller waits in a queue and the next free person picks up. Your staff can answer from wherever they are, on a desk phone or on the mobile app.

  4. 04

    Every call is logged and recorded

    Who called, when, who answered, how long it took. All of it goes into reports you can open whenever you like. Calls can be recorded too, so when a customer says "you promised me this", you can play it back and hear exactly what was said.

Quick glossary

The terms, in plain words

Six words you'll keep hearing about business phone systems, each explained in a sentence or two.

DID: your number

A phone number that lives in the cloud, not on a SIM card. It belongs to your business and stays with it for good, whether that's an 021, 042 or 051 city number.

Extension: one person

A short internal number for each person on the team. 101 is Ahmed, 102 is Sana. Callers reach the right person, and nobody needs a new SIM.

IVR: the greeting menu

The recorded voice that picks up the call and offers options: "press 1 for sales, press 2 for support". You can record it in Urdu, in English, or both.

Queue: a fair line

When everyone's busy, callers wait their turn with some hold music instead of getting a busy tone. Whoever frees up first takes the next caller.

Call recording

Every call is saved as an audio file you can play back later. Handy for training new staff, and for settling "who said what" without an argument.

Softphone: the app

An app on an ordinary smartphone that behaves like an office desk phone. Your team can make and take business calls from anywhere. It's your office, in your pocket.

The old way vs the Voipcom way

Most small businesses in Pakistan run on a pile of personal SIMs. Here's what changes when the phone system belongs to the business rather than to whoever happens to be holding the phone.

The old way

  • One SIM per employee, a drawer full of numbers, and a separate monthly bill for each one
  • When an employee leaves, the number leaves with them, and so do the customers who used to call it
  • No record of anything. Missed calls, promises and complaints all just disappear
  • The landline is stuck to a desk, so nobody answers after hours or from home

The Voipcom way

  • One business number with as many extensions as you need. Add a new person in minutes, no SIM required
  • The business owns the number. Staff come and go, and customers keep dialling the same one
  • Every call is logged and recorded, so reports show exactly who answered what, and when
  • The app puts your office in your pocket. Answer from the shop, from the car, or from home

Straight answers

Honest questions

The things business owners actually ask us, answered straight.

Do I have to buy new phones?

No. The app runs on the smartphones your team already carries. Desk phones are optional. Some owners like a nice one at reception, but nobody is going to make you buy hardware.

Does it need very fast internet?

No. Any stable 4G or broadband connection will do. A voice call uses less data than a WhatsApp voice note. What matters is a steady connection, not a fast one.

Can I keep my current number?

Bring it to the demo and we'll look at what's possible with that specific number. It varies case by case, and we'll give you the honest answer before you decide anything.

Can the greeting be in Urdu?

Yes. Record your IVR greeting in Urdu, in English, or in both. For example, a welcome in Urdu and then the menu options in English. You can change the recording whenever you like.

What if the internet goes down?

Calls can forward automatically to any mobile number you choose. Load shedding or a dead router shouldn't cost you a customer.

How long does setup take?

Usually one working day. We set up your number, greeting and extensions, then walk your team through the app. Honestly, most of your time goes into deciding what the IVR should say.

Does my staff need SIMs for this?

No. Extensions ring inside the app over the internet, so there's nothing to buy and no monthly package per employee. Your customers also never see anyone's personal number.

Setup times are typical rather than a firm promise, because every business is a little different. Ask us anything at the demo.
